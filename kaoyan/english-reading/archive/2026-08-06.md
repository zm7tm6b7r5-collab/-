## 4. 长难句逐句拆解（7 句）

### 句 1（P1）

**原句**：Yet, beneath that stability, entry-level roles are evolving faster than the post-secondary institutions that prepare graduates to fill them.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*entry-level roles are evolving*（主谓）
- 比较结构：faster than the post-secondary institutions（"快于……"）
- 定语从句：that prepare graduates to fill them（修饰 institutions，指"为毕业生填补这些岗位做准备的院校"）
- 状语：beneath that stability（"在这种稳定表象之下"）

**翻译**：然而，在这种稳定表象之下，入门级岗位的演变速度，已经快于那些为毕业生填补这些岗位做准备的中学后教育机构。

**语法点**：比较结构 + that 定语从句 + beneath 介词短语作状语；Yet 是典型转折衔接词（考研阅读高频）。

**仿写**：*Yet, beneath the surface of economic growth, the demand for new skills is evolving faster than the institutions that are supposed to prepare young people for the labour market.*（现象对比句，大作文首段）

### 句 2（P2）

**原句**：While this might eliminate some roles, others will be created – but these will demand something post-secondary institutions have long assumed they already teach: human judgement.

**结构拆解**：
- 让步状语从句：While this might eliminate some roles（"尽管这可能消灭一些岗位"）
- 主句：others will be created（被动语态）
- 转折并列：but these will demand something ...（对主句内容的递进转折）
- 定语从句（省略 that）：post-secondary institutions have long assumed they already teach（修饰 something）
- 宾语从句：they already teach（assumed 的宾语，省 that）
- 冒号解释：human judgement（点明"某种东西"是什么）

**翻译**：尽管这可能消灭一些岗位，其他岗位会被创造出来——但这些岗位将要求一种能力，而中学后教育机构长期以来一直以为自己在教的正是这种能力：人类判断力。

**语法点**：While 让步 + but 转折的"双转折"结构 + 省略 that 的双层宾语从句/定语从句 + 冒号定义。

**仿写**：*While automation might reduce some jobs, new ones will emerge – but these will require skills that traditional education has long assumed it already cultivates: critical thinking.*（科技与教育类作文）

### 句 3（P3）

**原句**：In an AI-enabled workplace, this is the "last mile": algorithms generate options but people decide what those options mean and what to do next.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*this is the "last mile"*（主系表）
- 冒号解释部分：algorithms generate options but people decide ...（解释"最后一公里"）
- 并列宾语从句：what those options mean（decide 的宾语①）
- 不定式宾语：what to do next（decide 的宾语②，疑问词+不定式）

**翻译**：在 AI 赋能的工作场所中，这就是"最后一公里"：算法生成选项，但由人来决定这些选项意味着什么、下一步该做什么。

**语法点**：冒号解释 + but 对比 + what 宾语从句与 what+不定式并列（疑问词+不定式是考研高频）。

**仿写**：*In an information-rich society, this is the real challenge: technology provides unlimited data, but people must decide what the data means and what to do with it.*（科技反思作文金句）

### 句 4（P4）

**原句**：The share of all mid- and entry-level job ads asking for human judgement has risen sharply over the past two years, with nearly half asking for skills in decision-making, problem-framing and accountability.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*The share ... has risen sharply*（主谓 + 程度状语）
- 后置定语：of all mid- and entry-level job ads（说明 share 的范围）
- 现在分词定语：asking for human judgement（修饰 job ads）
- 时间状语：over the past two years
- with 复合结构：with nearly half asking for skills ...（with + 名词 + 现在分词，补充数据）

**翻译**：过去两年间，要求"人类判断力"的中级与入门级招聘广告占比急剧上升，其中近一半还要求决策、问题界定与问责方面的技能。

**语法点**：现在分词作后置定语 + with 复合结构（独立主格）补充说明 + 连字符复合定语（mid- and entry-level）。

**仿写**：*The share of job ads requiring digital skills has risen sharply over the past five years, with nearly a third asking for data analysis and programming abilities.*（数据支撑类作文句）

### 句 5（P7）

**原句**：If human judgement is now a core labour market skill, why is it too often treated as an incidental outcome of higher education, rather than a deliberate one?

**结构拆解**：
- 条件状语从句：If human judgement is now a core labour market skill
- 主句（反问句）：why is it too often treated as an incidental outcome ...（被动语态 + 特殊疑问）
- 对比结构：rather than a deliberate one（"而非一个刻意的（培养结果）"，one 指代 outcome）

**翻译**：如果人类判断力如今已是劳动力市场的核心技能，为什么它常常只被当作高等教育的附带产物，而不是一个刻意为之的培养结果？

**语法点**：if 条件 + 反问句 + rather than 对比取舍 + one 指代（考研阅读态度题和完形高频）。

**仿写**：*If creativity is now a core workplace skill, why is it too often treated as an inborn talent, rather than a deliberate educational outcome?*（质疑现状的反问句，大作文第二段）

### 句 6（P10）

**原句**：That means designing coursework where students must operate under ambiguity: interpreting incomplete information, weighing competing constraints and making defensible decisions when there is no single correct answer.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*That means designing coursework*（主谓宾，动名词作宾语）
- 定语从句：where students must operate under ambiguity（修饰 coursework）
- 冒号解释部分：interpreting ..., weighing ... and making ...（三个现在分词并列，说明"如何在模糊中操作"）
- 时间/条件状语从句：when there is no single correct answer（修饰 making decisions）

**翻译**：这意味着要设计让学生在模糊情境下做决策的课程：解读不完整的信息、权衡相互冲突的约束条件、在没有唯一正确答案时做出经得起辩护的决定。

**语法点**：where 定语从句修饰抽象地点名词 + 冒号并列分词 + when 状语从句；operate under ambiguity 为高分搭配。

**仿写**：*That means designing training where employees must act under uncertainty: interpreting incomplete data, balancing conflicting demands and making timely decisions when there is no perfect option.*（对策建议段）

### 句 7（P11）

**原句**：In a world of probabilistic systems, knowing when not to trust the machine is as important as knowing how to use it.

**结构拆解**：
- 状语：In a world of probabilistic systems（背景状语）
- 主语：knowing when not to trust the machine（动名词短语作主语，内含疑问词+不定式）
- 谓语：is as important as（比较结构）
- 比较对象：knowing how to use it（动名词短语，how+不定式）

**翻译**：在一个充满概率性系统的世界里，知道何时不该信任机器，与知道如何使用它同等重要。

**语法点**：动名词短语作主语 + as...as 对等比较 + 疑问词+不定式（when/how to do）；"not to trust"的否定不定式。

**仿写**：*In an era of abundant information, knowing when not to believe what you read is as important as knowing how to find it.*（信息素养类作文金句，可直接背）

- 题干：The word "incidental" (Para 7) most probably means ____.
- 正确项：secondary / not deliberately planned（附带的、非刻意安排的——与后文 rather than a deliberate one 形成对比，是上下文线索）

> 备选 · 态度题：作者对大学现状的态度为 critical/urging reform（P7–P8 质疑 + P10–P12 呼吁结构性调整），干扰项常设"hostile/dismissive"（过度——作者承认大学有传统，只是要求升级）。备选 · 推断题陷阱：由"薪资溢价"不能推出"所有判断力岗位都高薪"（绝对化推断）。

## 附录 · 原文（Times Higher Education, 2026-06-09）

The debate over artificial intelligence and jobs has become oddly binary: either machines will wipe out work or leave employment largely untouched. The evidence so far points somewhere in between. Employment has held up in aggregate in recent years, with no clear, broad-based decline attributable to AI. Yet, beneath that stability, entry-level roles are evolving faster than the post-secondary institutions that prepare graduates to fill them.

The routine, low-discretion tasks that used to be the training ground for early career workers are the easiest to automate. While this might eliminate some roles, others will be created – but these will demand something post-secondary institutions have long assumed they already teach: human judgement.

This is not a soft skill. It is the operational layer of decision-making: the ability to frame a problem, weigh competing constraints, assess risk and take responsibility for outcomes when the answer is not obvious. In an AI-enabled workplace, this is the "last mile": algorithms generate options but people decide what those options mean and what to do next.

Job postings show how quickly this shift is taking hold. The share of all mid- and entry-level job ads asking for human judgement has risen sharply over the past two years, with nearly half asking for skills in decision-making, problem-framing and accountability.

This demand cuts across sectors but is most visible in sectors where day-to-day decisions carry financial, safety or regulatory consequences, such as banking, energy, utilities and healthcare. The trend also extends to more hands-on roles. Construction sites, supply chains and clinical settings increasingly rely on workers who can interpret signals, manage trade-offs and respond in real time. Automation does not remove human involvement in these contexts; it raises the cost of poor judgement.

The market rewards this accordingly. Jobs requiring human judgement skills consistently offer higher wages, with the premium in roles tied to risk and accountability. Notably, the advantage is clearest for diploma and bachelor's graduates – the bulk of the entry-level workforce pipeline.

This creates an uncomfortable question for post-secondary education. If human judgement is now a core labour market skill, why is it too often treated as an incidental outcome of higher education, rather than a deliberate one?

Most programmes remain organised around knowledge transmission and technical proficiency. Assessment rewards accuracy, not reasoning. Students learn to produce answers, often with increasingly powerful AI tools, but are rarely required to defend decisions or reflect on consequences. The result is graduates who are technically capable but judgement-light.

The adjustment required is structural, not cosmetic.

First, human judgement needs to be embedded explicitly into curricula as a defined, assessable outcome – not left as a by-product of disciplinary learning. That means designing coursework where students must operate under ambiguity: interpreting incomplete information, weighing competing constraints and making defensible decisions when there is no single correct answer. The assessment of reasoning quality is more important than technical accuracy.

Second, AI literacy must be paired with oversight. Teaching students how to use AI tools is insufficient. They need to understand when to question outputs, how to identify bias or error, and where accountability ultimately sits. In a world of probabilistic systems, knowing when not to trust the machine is as important as knowing how to use it.

Finally, experiential learning needs teeth. Work-integrated learning often places students in observational or execution roles, shielded from decision-making. This is a missed opportunity. Placements should include scoped responsibility: assessing options, identifying risks, making recommendations and reflecting on outcomes. Judgement develops through exposure to consequences, not just tasks.

None of this is entirely new. Universities have long claimed to teach critical thinking, and colleges have long emphasised applied learning. What is new is the clarity of the demand for it.

The ladder is still there for junior employees but the first rung is higher. Graduates are expected to arrive not just with knowledge but with the capacity to make decisions. In that sense, the future of work is not less human but the bar is raised on what only humans can do. Post-secondary institutions will have to work ever harder to keep up.
