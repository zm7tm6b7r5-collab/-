**全文论证结构图**：

```
引入（现象：呼吁伦理保障）
  → 背景（同意程序过时）
  → 科普（生物计算机是什么、为何受追捧）
  → 转折（捐献者预期错位）
  → 论据（历史教训 + 调查态度）
```

**论证模式总结**：典型的"问题—分析—建议"结构，即 **现象引入 → 技术背景 → 问题深化 → 论据支撑 → 对策 → 结论**。考研阅读中此类文章的高频命题点集中在 P4 的转折、P8 的"分级方案"细节、P9 的作者态度。

## 4. 长难句逐句拆解（7 句）

### 句 1（P1）

**原句**：Researchers developing computers powered by lab-grown brain tissue are calling for stronger ethical safeguards, warning that many people who donate cells for medical research may not realize their tissue could eventually be used in biocomputing, Qazinform News Agency correspondent reports.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*Researchers ... are calling for stronger ethical safeguards*（主语 + 谓语 + 宾语）
- 后置定语①：developing computers...（现在分词短语修饰 Researchers，即"研发计算机的研究人员"）
- 后置定语②：powered by lab-grown brain tissue（过去分词短语修饰 computers，表被动）
- 伴随状语：warning that ...（现在分词短语，伴随"呼吁"同时发出警告）
- 宾语从句①：that many people ... may not realize ...（warning 的宾语）
- 定语从句：who donate cells for medical research（修饰 people）
- 宾语从句②：their tissue could eventually be used in biocomputing（realize 的宾语，省略 that）
- 插入语：Qazinform News Agency correspondent reports（报道来源标注）

**翻译**：据 Qazinform 通讯社记者报道，正在研发由实验室培养的人脑组织驱动的计算机的研究人员呼吁建立更严格的伦理保障，并警告说，许多为医学研究捐献细胞的人可能没有意识到，他们的组织最终可能被用于生物计算。

**语法点**：分词作定语 + 分词作状语 + 多层宾语从句嵌套（典型的考研真题首句结构）。

**仿写**（用于科技类作文开头）：*Researchers developing new technologies often call for stronger regulation, warning that the public may not fully grasp the risks.*（研发新技术的科研人员常常呼吁加强监管，警告公众可能并未完全理解其中的风险。）

### 句 2（P2）

**原句**：According to an article published in Nature, scientists say current consent procedures do not clearly explain that donated cells could one day help create brain-based computers, including technologies with commercial applications.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*scientists say*（后接宾语从句）
- 状语：According to an article published in Nature（介词短语；published in Nature 为过去分词定语修饰 article）
- 宾语从句①：(that) current consent procedures do not clearly explain ...（say 的宾语，省略 that）
- 宾语从句②：that donated cells could one day help create brain-based computers（explain 的宾语）
- 补充说明：including technologies with commercial applications（分词短语补充 brain-based computers 的范围）

**翻译**：据一篇发表在《自然》杂志上的文章，科学家们表示，目前的知情同意程序并未清楚说明：捐献的细胞有一天可能帮助制造基于大脑的计算机，其中包括具有商业应用的技术。

**语法点**：宾语从句嵌套 + including 分词作补充说明。

### 句 3（P3）

**原句**：Researchers believe these systems could one day process information more efficiently than conventional silicon-based computers while using far less energy.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*Researchers believe*（后接宾语从句）
- 宾语从句：these systems could one day process information more efficiently than conventional silicon-based computers（省略了与主句重复的 could process information）
- 伴随/时间状语：while using far less energy（= while they are using far less energy，while + 现在分词省略主语和 be）

**翻译**：研究人员认为，这些系统有朝一日能够比传统硅基计算机更高效地处理信息，同时耗能要少得多。

**语法点**：比较结构省略 + while + 现在分词（省略主语）表伴随，是外刊高频写法。

**仿写**（用于对比类作文）：*Remote work allows employees to complete tasks more flexibly than office-based arrangements while cutting commuting time.*（远程办公让员工比坐班更灵活地完成任务，同时减少了通勤时间。）

### 句 4（P4）

**原句**：However, the authors say many donors likely expected their samples to support health research, such as developing treatments for disease, rather than engineering or computer science projects.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*the authors say*（后接宾语从句）
- 宾语从句：many donors likely expected their samples to support health research（expect + 宾语 + 不定式结构）
- 举例：such as developing treatments for disease
- 对比结构：rather than engineering or computer science projects（"而非……"，表取舍）

**翻译**：然而，作者们表示，许多捐献者原本很可能期望自己的样本用于健康研究（例如开发疾病治疗方法），而不是用于工程或计算机科学项目。

**语法点**：expect sb/sth to do sth 结构 + rather than 对比取舍（考研完形与阅读高频考点）。

### 句 5（P5）

**原句**：They point to past cases in which donated biological samples were later used in ways that participants had not anticipated, leading to legal disputes and a loss of trust.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*They point to past cases*
- 定语从句①：in which donated biological samples were later used in ways（in which = in the cases，修饰 cases）
- 定语从句②：that participants had not anticipated（修饰 ways，注意用过去完成时表示"此前未预料"）
- 结果状语：leading to legal disputes and a loss of trust（现在分词短语，表前因后果）

**翻译**：他们列举了过去的案例：在这些案例中，捐献的生物样本后来被以参与者未曾预料的方式使用，最终导致法律纠纷和信任的丧失。

**语法点**：介词 + 关系代词（in which）引导定语从句 + 定语从句套定语从句 + 分词作结果状语，是考研最难句型的组合之一。

### 句 6（P8）

**原句**：Only when neither option is feasible should an independent ethics committee determine whether existing samples can be used.

**结构拆解**：
- 主句：*an independent ethics committee should determine ...*（部分倒装：Only + 状语从句置于句首时，助动词 should 提前到主语前）
- 时间/条件状语从句：when neither option is feasible（neither 表"两者都不"）
- 宾语从句：whether existing samples can be used
- 正常语序还原：An independent ethics committee should determine whether existing samples can be used only when neither option is feasible.

**翻译**：只有当上述两种方案都不可行时，独立的伦理委员会才应决定现有样本是否可以使用。

**语法点**：**Only + 状语从句 → 主句部分倒装**，考研语法高频考点，也是写作加分句型。

**仿写**（用于建议类作文结尾）：*Only when legal protection and public education work together can the risks of new technologies be controlled.*（只有当法律保护与公众教育协同发力时，新技术的风险才能得到控制。）

### 句 7（P9）

**原句**：Even so, they argue that investing in clearer consent procedures now will help maintain public trust while allowing brain-based computing technologies to develop responsibly.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*they argue that ...*
- 宾语从句：investing in clearer consent procedures now will help maintain public trust（动名词短语 investing in ... 作从句主语）
- 伴随状语：while allowing brain-based computing technologies to develop responsibly（while + 现在分词，表"同时"）

**翻译**：即便如此，他们仍主张，现在投入建立更清晰的知情同意程序，将有助于维护公众信任，同时让基于大脑的计算技术得以负责任地发展。

**语法点**：动名词短语作主语 + help (to) do + while + 现在分词；"Even so" 是典型的让步衔接词。

## 附录 · 原文

Researchers developing computers powered by lab-grown brain tissue are calling for stronger ethical safeguards, warning that many people who donate cells for medical research may not realize their tissue could eventually be used in biocomputing, Qazinform News Agency correspondent reports.

According to an article published in Nature, scientists say current consent procedures do not clearly explain that donated cells could one day help create brain-based computers, including technologies with commercial applications. They argue that existing rules should be updated before the field advances further.

Brain-based biocomputers use tiny clusters of brain cells, known as brain organoids, grown from human stem cells. Researchers believe these systems could one day process information more efficiently than conventional silicon-based computers while using far less energy. Early experiments have already shown that brain organoids can perform simple computing tasks.

Most of the cells used in this research come from volunteers who donated tissue for medical or biological studies. However, the authors say many donors likely expected their samples to support health research, such as developing treatments for disease, rather than engineering or computer science projects.

The researchers argue that broad consent, which allows donated samples to be used in future biomedical research, may not be sufficient for biocomputing. They point to past cases in which donated biological samples were later used in ways that participants had not anticipated, leading to legal disputes and a loss of trust.

Studies have also found that some donors are particularly concerned about research involving brain organoids, especially when commercial uses are involved. Some have said they would like to know how their cells are being used or have the option to withdraw consent if they disagree with future projects.

To address these concerns, the authors recommend creating independent oversight committees dedicated to biocomputing research. These groups would review both the ethical implications of projects and whether existing cell lines can be used appropriately.

The researchers propose three preferred approaches. The first is to establish new stem cell lines from donors who give explicit consent for biocomputing research. If that is not possible, scientists should seek renewed consent from people who previously donated tissue. Only when neither option is feasible should an independent ethics committee determine whether existing samples can be used.

The authors acknowledge that obtaining fresh consent may be difficult, particularly for older samples when donors cannot be identified or contacted. Even so, they argue that investing in clearer consent procedures now will help maintain public trust while allowing brain-based computing technologies to develop responsibly.
