## 4. 长难句逐句拆解（7 句）

### 句 1（P2）

**原句**：By 2050, Europe is expected to have 60 million more pensioners than it does today, supported by a smaller working-age population and financed in large part by a younger generation that currently owns less than 5% of the continent's wealth.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*Europe is expected to have 60 million more pensioners*（被动语态 + 比较结构）
- 比较从句：than it does today（省略 have）
- 并列分词状语①：supported by a smaller working-age population（过去分词表被动，说明养老金由谁支撑）
- 并列分词状语②：financed in large part by a younger generation ...（过去分词，说明资金来自年轻一代）
- 定语从句：that currently owns less than 5% of the continent's wealth（修饰 generation）

**翻译**：到 2050 年，预计欧洲的养老金领取者将比现在多 6000 万，由更小的劳动年龄人口支撑，资金在很大程度上来自目前只拥有欧洲不到 5% 财富的年轻一代。

**语法点**：被动语态 + 比较省略 + 并列过去分词状语（supported/financed）+ that 定语从句；"数字+被动+分词"是外刊数据句的经典模板。

**仿写**：*By 2035, the number of elderly citizens is expected to grow far faster than the working-age population, supported by shrinking families and financed in large part by a younger generation that already faces rising housing costs.*（人口议题数据句）

### 句 2（P3）

**原句**：If young people are asked to pay more, work longer, save less and delay key life milestones, while doubting whether the systems they are paying into will still be there for them, then ageing becomes more than a fiscal issue.

**结构拆解**：
- 条件状语从句：If young people are asked to pay more, work longer, save less and delay key life milestones（四个并列不定式）
- 伴随状语：while doubting whether ...（while + 现在分词）
- 宾语从句：whether the systems ... will still be there for them（doubting 的宾语）
- 定语从句（省略 that）：they are paying into（修饰 systems，介词 into 前置关系）
- 主句：then ageing becomes more than a fiscal issue（more than 强调"不只是"）

**翻译**：如果年轻人被要求多缴钱、工作更久、储蓄更少、推迟人生关键节点，同时又在怀疑自己正在缴费的制度未来是否还会存在，那么老龄化就不仅仅是一个财政问题。

**语法点**：if 条件 + while+现在分词伴随 + whether 宾语从句 + 省略 that 的定语从句（pay into）+ more than 强调——条件句嵌套的典型考句。

**仿写**：*If young workers are asked to pay higher taxes and save more for retirement, while doubting whether the system will still support them, then the policy becomes more than an economic issue.*（政策评论段）

### 句 3（P4）

**原句**：This is a generation defined less by ideology than by practical concerns, cautious optimism and a growing sense that Europe's social contract is under strain.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*This is a generation*（主系表）
- 过去分词定语：defined less by ideology than by practical concerns ...（less...than 比较结构："与其说……不如说……"）
- 并列名词短语：practical concerns, cautious optimism and a growing sense（than by 后的三个并列对象）
- 同位语从句：that Europe's social contract is under strain（解释 sense）

**翻译**：这一代人与其说是由意识形态界定的，不如说是由现实关切、审慎的乐观以及一种越来越强烈的感觉所界定——那就是欧洲的社会契约正承受着压力。

**语法点**：less...than 比较结构（考研高频）+ 过去分词定语 + 并列宾语 + 同位语从句；under strain 为高分搭配。

**仿写**：*This is a generation defined less by political labels than by economic insecurity, environmental anxiety and a growing sense that the old promises no longer hold.*（群体特征描述句，人物/代际类作文）

### 句 4（P5）

**原句**：On healthcare, present access is overshadowed by fears that ageing societies will stretch already pressured systems even further.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*present access is overshadowed by fears*（被动语态）
- 同位语从句：that ageing societies will stretch already pressured systems even further（解释 fears 的具体内容）
- 后置定语：already pressured systems（过去分词作定语修饰 systems）

**翻译**：在医疗方面，当前的（服务）可及性被这样一种担忧所笼罩：老龄化社会将把本已承压的体系进一步拖垮。

**语法点**：被动语态 + 同位语从句（fears that ...）+ 过去分词定语；overshadow 为比喻用法"使黯然、笼罩"。

**仿写**：*On education, present opportunities are overshadowed by fears that rapid technological change will make current skills outdated even faster.*（分主题论述段）

### 句 5（P6）

**原句**：In one, ageing deepens the gap between generations: younger Europeans shoulder rising tax and care burdens while trusting institutions less and less.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*ageing deepens the gap between generations*（主谓宾）
- 冒号解释部分：younger Europeans shoulder rising tax and care burdens ...（说明"代际裂痕"的具体表现）
- 伴随状语：while trusting institutions less and less（while + 现在分词）

**翻译**：在一种未来中，老龄化加深代际鸿沟：欧洲年轻人一边扛起不断上升的税收与照护负担，一边对制度的信任越来越少。

**语法点**：冒号解释 + while+现在分词表同时进行；shoulder 动词化用法"承担"。

**仿写**：*In one scenario, automation widens the income gap: low-skilled workers shoulder the burden of displacement while watching the benefits flow to a few.*（展望对比段）

### 句 6（P8）

**原句**：That means being honest about what change will require, and it means no longer treating demographic ageing as a background trend.

**结构拆解**：
- 主干：*That means being honest about ...*（动名词作宾语）
- 宾语从句：what change will require（about 的宾语，what 引导）
- 并列主干：and it means no longer treating demographic ageing as a background trend（动名词 + treat...as 结构）

**翻译**：这意味着要诚实地面对变革将要求什么，也意味着不再把人口老龄化当作一种背景趋势。

**语法点**：并列动名词宾语 + what 宾语从句 + treat A as B 结构；no longer 的否定语义。

**仿写**：*That means being honest about what the reform will cost, and it means no longer treating the housing crisis as a temporary problem.*（呼吁行动段）

### 句 7（P8）

**原句**：If they succeed, ageing can still become a moment of renewal rather than decline.

**结构拆解**：
- 条件状语从句：If they succeed（they 指代欧洲领导人）
- 主干：*ageing can still become a moment of renewal*（主系表）
- 对比结构：rather than decline（"而非衰退"）

**翻译**：如果他们成功了，老龄化仍可以成为一个复兴的时刻，而非衰退的时刻。

**语法点**：if 条件 + rather than 对比取舍（考研完形与写作高频）。

**仿写**：*If policymakers respond wisely, an ageing society can still become a source of renewal rather than decline.*（政策立场收尾句）

## 7. 今日学习建议

**词汇复习（结合例句自测）**：pensioner、fiscal、social contract、under strain、undercut、catalyst、erosion、renewal、demographic。重点：social contract（社会契约）在政治学/社会学语境的双重含义；undercut 的"暗中削弱"义与 undermine 同义替换。

**可直接用于作文的句型**：

## 附录 · 原文（Philea / Debating Europe, 2026-06-19）

Over the last couple of years, I have become slightly obsessed with how Europe's population is ageing. Part of that is professional. Much of my work involves listening to citizens and trying to understand the choices shaping Europe. But part of it is also personal. As a parent, I cannot help thinking about the kind of future today's choices are creating for my kids.

When we look at the numbers, the scale is hard to ignore. By 2050, Europe is expected to have 60 million more pensioners than it does today, supported by a smaller working-age population and financed in large part by a younger generation that currently owns less than 5% of the continent's wealth. That is not just a future demographic challenge. It is already deeply personal for young Europeans, shaping their present through unaffordable housing, insecure work, stretched public services, uncertain pensions and strained healthcare.

It also raises a deeper democratic question: what happens to trust when younger generations feel the system is no longer working for them? If young people are asked to pay more, work longer, save less and delay key life milestones, while doubting whether the systems they are paying into will still be there for them, then ageing becomes more than a fiscal issue. It becomes a test of trust and fairness between generations.

Our latest report, Voices for Choices 2026, listens to 2,000 young people across Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and Spain. Their concerns point to one central question: is Europe ageing fairly? Across the six countries, a clear picture emerges. This is a generation defined less by ideology than by practical concerns, cautious optimism and a growing sense that Europe's social contract is under strain.

That strain runs through all five themes of the report. On pensions, young people are paying into systems they do not fully trust and increasingly expect to work longer for less certainty. On housing, rising costs are delaying independence, family formation and long-term security. On healthcare, present access is overshadowed by fears that ageing societies will stretch already pressured systems even further. On education, many still see learning as a route to opportunity, but doubt current systems are preparing them for longer and more uncertain working lives. On work, optimism about finding jobs is repeatedly undercut by low pay, instability and the feeling that labour no longer guarantees independence.

Taken together, these pressures point to two possible futures. In one, ageing deepens the gap between generations: younger Europeans shoulder rising tax and care burdens while trusting institutions less and less. In the other, ageing becomes the catalyst for renewing Europe's social contract, forcing policymakers to treat pensions, housing, healthcare, education and work as connected foundations of social stability and democratic trust.

I hope we choose the second path.

That means being honest about what change will require, and it means no longer treating demographic ageing as a background trend. It's a structural challenge, but also a political choice. If Europe's leaders fail to respond, the result will be more than policy drift. It will be a deeper erosion of trust between generations. If they succeed, ageing can still become a moment of renewal rather than decline.
