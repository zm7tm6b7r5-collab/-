## 三、段落逻辑梳理

> 导语：这项技术注定在战争中扮演更大角色，但伦理挑战悬而未决。

**P1｜引入现象**
- 大意：未来的 AI 无人机应否拥有"杀人执照"？政府和国防工业已承认无人机将在未来战争中扮演关键角色，问题日益紧迫。
- 段间衔接：开篇设问（"Should... have a licence to kill?"）直接立题。
- 论证作用：提出问题——道德能否内置进武器系统。

**P2｜背景交代**
- 大意：乌克兰战场大量使用无人机、伊朗冲突中 AI 协助轰炸，观察者预期武器将获得更高"作战自主性"，这意味着需要某种近似道德的框架。
- 段间衔接："With... being deployed... and AI being used..." 用独立主格交代现实背景。
- 论证作用：说明"为什么现在必须回答道德问题"。

**P3｜反方观点（学界/业界）**
- 大意：微软 AI 负责人、DeepMind 联合创始人 Suleyman 态度明确："AI 不能是人——也不是道德存在。"
- 段间衔接：直接引语，立场鲜明的反对者登场。
- 论证作用：给出"机器不可能有道德"的强立场。

**P4｜正方观点（官方）**
- 大意：前 GCHQ 负责人 Omand 认为 AI 可以为无人武器配置"道德"方案；英国武装部队大臣 Carns 表示必须有"把人移出决策回路"的选项——核心挑战就此成形。
- 段间衔接："...has told the Guardian... while... told the Financial Times..." 以 while 并列官方两种表态。
- 论证作用：官方立场与学界立场对撞，冲突成立。

**P5｜反方观点（学者）**
- 大意：爱丁堡大学 Talat 认为，支撑聊天机器人的大语言模型从根本上无法进行道德决策。
- 段间衔接：承接 P4 的"挑战"下定义（"argues that..."）。
- 论证作用：把争论拉回技术机制层面。

**P6｜机制解释**
- 大意：AI 靠海量数据建立"下一个最可能的词/句"的概率模型，这与人类的道德推理不同，即使模型读遍人类哲学著作也一样。
- 段间衔接：无转折词，解释上一段结论的原理。
- 论证作用：从机制上否定"概率模型=道德能力"。

**P7｜引语+论文支撑**
- 大意：Talat 反问"道德会遵循概率观念吗"；其合著论文认为伦理评价是"开放、辩论式、社会政治过程"，AI 跟不上。
- 段间衔接：直接引语承接机制分析，论文作证据。
- 论证作用：学术证据强化反方。

**P8｜中性评估（技术进展）**
- 大意：萨里大学 Rogoyski 承认自 ChatGPT 以来 AI 模型进步很大（如"推理模型"），但仍怀疑能否复刻道德决策的细微之处。
- 段间衔接："Nonetheless" 转折：先进 ≠ 能道德推理。
- 论证作用：为"反方"补充相对公允的中间判断。

**P9｜引语（核心断言）**
- 大意：道德"深刻、复杂、充满争议、由文化塑造"，多数人终其一生也未必想清楚；我们连自己都说不清的东西，不能指望机器先具备。
- 段间衔接：直接引语承接上一句疑问。
- 论证作用："无法编码就无法内置"——全文最有力的反方论点之一。

**P10｜国际法视角**
- 大意：乌得勒支大学国际法学者 Dorsey 提出"这架无人机遵循谁的道德"；联合国至今未就自主武器治理达成全球共识。
- 段间衔接："This leads to the question of whether..." 由技术转入规范层面。
- 论证作用：指出"普遍道德准则"不存在。

**P11｜法律细节**
- 大意：另一个难题是区分战斗员与平民——日内瓦公约第 57 条要求攻击前"尽一切可行手段核实目标非平民/民用物体"。
- 段间衔接："There is also the issue of..." 递进列举法律障碍。
- 论证作用：给出具体法条，论证"内置道德"的合规难度。

**P12｜风险警示**
- 大意：Dorsey 警告：法律没弄对，AI 无人机大规模部署时会以更大规模重复错误决定。
- 段间衔接：直接引语承接法条分析，落到后果。
- 论证作用：把抽象伦理问题变成可量化的风险。

**P13｜引语（风险放大）**
- 大意：战争中变量太多、出错是必然；而错误以"AI 速度"发生时，难以挽回。
- 段间衔接：引语递进强化。
- 论证作用：以"速度"这个关键词呼应 velocity gap 热词。

**P14｜转折（反方立场）**
- 大意：但也有专家认为，只要别国继续以同样速度研发部署，赋予无人机更大自主性并内置交战规则与道德就是"必须"。
- 段间衔接："But some experts argue..." 观点转向正方。
- 论证作用：引入"军备竞赛逻辑"，形成完整对立。

**P15｜引语（必要性论证）**
- 大意：神经科学家 Wright 称，要与其他高端军队有效对抗，就需要大量能自主决策的系统。
- 段间衔接：直接引语承接"necessity"。
- 论证作用：从军事竞争角度为自主武器辩护。

**P16｜现状交代（行业分裂）**
- 大意：AI 无人机仍是新兴技术、实战案例有限；美欧 100 多家初创公司在做无人机与软件平台，对未来决策方式的看法尖锐对立。
- 段间衔接：由宏观争论转到行业现场（"More than 100 startups..."）。
- 论证作用：揭示"技术界内部也没有共识"。

**P17｜行业观点 A（Hichwa）**

- 段间衔接：引语开篇，行业观点 A 登场。

**P18｜观点 A 续**

- 段间衔接：无转折词，补充说明立场。
- 论证作用：明确"增强而非替代"的产品哲学。

**P19｜引语（批评方向）**
- 大意：很多人把自主性看错了——不从使用者视角出发，只想着"全自主武器系统"。
- 段间衔接：引语承接观点 A。
- 论证作用：点出行业内"全自主"叙事的误导性。

**P20｜引语（现实判断）**
- 大意：战争的现实是，它仍是"人与人"的互动。
- 段间衔接：引语递进收束。
- 论证作用：为"人留决策权"提供战争本质论据。

**P21｜用例（认知减负）**

- 段间衔接："One use of AI in warfare, he says, might be to..." 举例展开。

**P22｜引语（自主示例）**

## 四、长难句逐句拆解（6 句）

**S1**
- 原句：With drones being deployed in huge numbers in the Ukraine war and AI being used to assist bombing missions in the Iran conflict, there is an expectation among some observers that weapons will have to operate with increased operational autonomy, which means they will need something approximating a moral framework.
- 结构拆解：With + 两个独立主格（drones being deployed... and AI being used...）作背景状语 → there is an expectation that（同位语从句）→ which 非限制性定语从句补充说明。
- 翻译：随着乌克兰战争中大量部署无人机、伊朗冲突中 AI 被用于协助轰炸任务，一些观察者预期武器将不得不以更高的作战自主性运行，这意味着它们需要某种近似于道德的框架。
- 语法点：with 复合结构（独立主格）；同位语从句；which 定语从句。
- 仿写：With remote work becoming the norm and AI assistants handling routine tasks, there is a growing expectation among managers that employees will have to develop new collaboration skills, which means training budgets will rise.

**S2**
- 原句：AI systems are trained on vast amounts of data that allow them to build a probabilistic model of what is the next most likely word or sentence in a sequence.
- 结构拆解：主句（AI systems are trained...）→ that 定语从句（allow them to build...）→ of what 宾语从句（what is the next most likely word...）。
- 翻译：AI 系统在海量数据上训练，得以建立"序列中下一个最可能出现的词或句子是什么"的概率模型。
- 语法点：that 定语从句；介词 + what 从句。
- 仿写：Language apps are trained on millions of conversations that allow them to build a probabilistic model of what a user is most likely to say next.

**S3**
- 原句："If you have a machine that's probabilistic by nature it will veer towards the most likely answer in a situation. Do we think that morality follows probabilistic notions?" says Talat, who has recently co-authored a paper that argues ethical evaluation is an "open-ended, debate-based, sociopolitical process", which AI cannot keep up with.
- 结构拆解：直接引语（If 条件句 + 主句 + 反问）→ says Talat → who 定语从句（co-authored a paper that argues...）→ which 定语从句（介词 with 置于句末，= which AI cannot keep up with）。
- 翻译："如果一台机器本质上是概率性的，它在情境中就会滑向最可能的答案。我们真的认为道德遵循概率观念吗？"Talat 说。他最近与人合著论文，认为伦理评价是一种"开放的、基于辩论的社会政治过程"，AI 无法跟上。
- 语法点：引语后置主谓；多重定语从句嵌套；keep up with 中介词置于从句末尾。
- 仿写："If an algorithm is trained on biased data, it will amplify the dominant pattern rather than question it," says Liu, who has recently published a paper that argues fairness is a context-dependent process which machines cannot simply compute.

**S4**
- 原句：Her concerns include determining whose morality the drone is following, a difficult process given the United Nations is still trying to achieve a global consensus on autonomous weapons governance.
- 结构拆解：主句（Her concerns include determining...）→ whose 宾语从句 → a difficult process 作同位语 → given（= given that）引导条件/原因从句。
- 翻译：她的担忧包括确定"无人机遵循的是谁的道德"——鉴于联合国仍在努力就自主武器治理达成全球共识，这本身就是一个艰难的过程。
- 语法点：whose 宾语从句；同位语；given 引导从句（given that 的省略形式）。
- 仿写：Her concerns include deciding whose interests the policy serves, a delicate task given the public still disagrees about the goal of reform.

**S5**
- 原句：Morality is the province of human beings, which is why AI-assisted weapons systems need to be built in a way that extends the judgment and decision-making of the operator rather than wholesale replacing it.
- 结构拆解：主句（Morality is the province of human beings）→ which 非限制性定语从句指代整个主句 → why 表语从句 → that 定语从句修饰 way → rather than 对比结构。
- 翻译：道德属于人类，这就是为什么 AI 辅助武器系统必须以"延伸操作者的判断与决策、而非整体取代它"的方式来构建。
- 语法点：which 指代前句；why 表语从句；rather than + 动名词。
- 仿写：Teaching is a human craft, which is why education tools should be designed in a way that extends the teacher's judgement rather than replacing it.

**S6**
- 原句：Alex Fink, the US chief executive of Swarmer, a US-Ukraine startup that develops software for autonomous drones, says there could be a future scenario where humans might not select individual targets at all but instead designate a "kill box", in which anything in a given area at a given time is fair game for an autonomous system to target.
- 结构拆解：主语 + 双同位语（the US chief executive of Swarmer + a US-Ukraine startup that...）→ says + 宾语从句（there could be a future scenario where...）→ in which 定语从句。
- 翻译：Swarmer（一家开发自主无人机软件的美国-乌克兰初创公司）的美国首席执行官 Alex Fink 表示，未来可能出现这样的场景：人类根本不再逐个选择目标，而是划定一个"击杀盒"——在指定时间、指定区域内，任何事物都可以被自主系统视为合法攻击目标。
- 语法点：双同位语；where 定语从句；in which 介词+关系代词；fair game 熟词僻义。
- 仿写：The new policy, a nationwide pilot project that covers ten cities, could create a situation where local governments might not design individual solutions at all but instead follow a standard template, in which every district is expected to copy the same model.

## 六、命题角度预测（4 题）

**Q1 主旨题**
- 题干：Which of the following best captures the main idea of the passage?
- 正确项：Whether morality can be programmed into autonomous weapons remains unresolved, and technology is outpacing moral and legal consensus.
- 干扰项逻辑：
  - A（无人机已广泛投入实战并自主开火）：文中明确 "limited examples of their deployment"；
  - B（学界一致反对自主武器）：Omand、Wright 等持支持/必要论；
  - D（国际社会已就自主武器达成规则）：联合国仍在寻求共识。

**Q2 细节题**
- 题干：According to Jessica Dorsey, one problem with programming morality into drones is ______.
- 正确项：it is unclear whose morality the drone would follow
- 干扰项逻辑：
  - A（无人机无法承受道德计算的算力）：无中生有；
  - C（飞行员无法掌握自主武器）：与她的论证无关；
  - D（各国已禁止自主武器）：联合国尚未达成共识。

**Q3 推断题**
- 题干：Zee Talat would most likely agree that ______.
- 正确项：large language models cannot make moral decisions because probabilistic prediction is different from ethical reasoning
- 干扰项逻辑：
  - A（模型读遍哲学著作后就能道德判断）：原文明确否定；
  - C（伦理评价可以自动化为固定程序）：其论文称伦理是"开放、辩论式过程"；
  - D（应让人工智能完全接管武器决策）：与其观点相反。

**Q4 词义题**
- 题干：In the context of paragraph 26, "fair game" most nearly means ______.
- 正确项：a legitimate target for attack
- 干扰项逻辑：
  - B（公平竞赛）：望文生义，忽略军事语境；
  - C（平等机会）：无中生有；
  - D（受保护对象）：与"可被自主系统攻击"相反。

## 七、今日学习建议

- 重点复习词（6–8 个）：unequivocal、probabilistic、codify、combatant、nascent、cognitive burden、fair game、consensus。建议把 "take the human out of the loop" 作为"人机关系"话题的核心习语单独记。
- 作文句型：
  1. "This leads to the question of whether..."——承上启下的设问。仿写：This leads to the question of whether stricter regulation will actually reduce online harm.
  2. "Morality is the province of human beings, which is why..."——"X 属于人类，因此……"论证人机边界。仿写：Creativity is the province of human beings, which is why AI tools should assist rather than replace writers.
  3. "But if the technology is there, the ... is not."——对比收束句。仿写：But if the demand is there, the infrastructure is not.
- 鼓励性反馈：这篇是"伦理辩论"的教科书写法——同一问题给出官方、学界、国际法、行业三方五派立场，最后一句点题。你抓住"谁支持、谁反对、凭什么"，这类文章就赢了一半。

## 附录：原文全文（文字版）

**Can autonomous AI-powered killer drones take morality onboard?**

By Dan Milmo and Aisha Down, The Guardian, June 3, 2026

While the technology is set to play a growing role in modern warfare, there remains an unresolved ethical challenge

Should the AI-powered drones of the future have a licence to kill? The question is becoming ever more pressing as governments and the defence industry acknowledge that drone systems will play an increasingly crucial role in future warfare.

With drones being deployed in huge numbers in the Ukraine war and AI being used to assist bombing missions in the Iran conflict, there is an expectation among some observers that weapons will have to operate with increased operational autonomy, which means they will need something approximating a moral framework.

Last year Mustafa Suleyman, chief executive of Microsoft's AI arm and a co-founder of the UK-based DeepMind, was unequivocal about the issue of machines making moral decisions. He said: "AIs cannot be people – or moral beings."

David Omand, the former head of the UK spy agency, GCHQ, has told the Guardian he believes AI can create a "moral" configuration for unmanned weapons, while the UK armed forces minister, Al Carns, told the Financial Times recently there must be an option to "take the human out of the loop" in decision-making. This brings into focus the ethical and technological challenge of whether morality can be programmed into an autonomous weapons system.

Zee Talat, an academic specialising in machine learning at the University of Edinburgh's school of informatics, argues that large language models – the technology that underpins modern generative AI systems such as chatbots – are fundamentally incapable of moral decision-making.

AI systems are trained on vast amounts of data that allow them to build a probabilistic model of what is the next most likely word or sentence in a sequence. This is not how humans make moral and ethical decisions, says Talat, even if the model has ingested all the philosophical tracts known to humanity.

"If you have a machine that's probabilistic by nature it will veer towards the most likely answer in a situation. Do we think that morality follows probabilistic notions?" says Talat, who has recently co-authored a paper that argues ethical evaluation is an "open-ended, debate-based, sociopolitical process", which AI cannot keep up with.

Andrew Rogoyski, of the Institute for People-Centred AI at the University of Surrey, says AI systems have become much more sophisticated since the arrival of ChatGPT in 2022 – as the emergence of so-called "reasoning" models shows. Nonetheless, can they replicate the nuance of moral decision-making?

"Morality is deeply complex, contested, culturally shaped, and something most humans never fully resolve, even for themselves," he says. "Perhaps the real question is whether we understand morality well enough to codify it. Until we do, we cannot expect machines to embody something we ourselves cannot clearly articulate."

This leads to the question of whether it is possible to have a universally recognised moral code for autonomous weapons. Programming morality into drones is problematic, according to Jessica Dorsey, an assistant professor of international law at Utrecht University in the Netherlands. Her concerns include determining whose morality the drone is following, a difficult process given the United Nations is still trying to achieve a global consensus on autonomous weapons governance.

There is also the issue of how to programme an AI system to distinguish between a combatant and a civilian. Article 57 of the Geneva conventions – a series of international treaties regulating the conduct of war – states that combatants should do "everything feasible to verify that the objectives to be attacked are neither civilians nor civilian objects … but are military objectives".

If you don't get the law right you will end up repeating flawed decisions on a vast scale if AI-powered drones are deployed en masse, says Dorsey.

"War is filled with so many variables and it is a given that things will go wrong. And when that happens at AI-like speed, it is difficult to unravel," she says.

But some experts argue that giving drones greater autonomy, and programming rules of engagement and morality into them, will be a necessity if other nation states continue to develop and deploy similar technology at pace.

"For any military to compete effectively against other high-end militaries it is going to need a large amount of systems that will be required to take decisions on their own," says Nicholas Wright, a neuroscientist and author of Warhead, a book on the human brain and war.

AI-powered drones are still a nascent technology, and, despite tech industry hype, there are only limited examples of their deployment on the battlefield. More than 100 startups across the US and Europe are now building drones and drone software platforms. These range from light, low-altitude drones designed for imaging and surveillance to heavier craft designed to carry weapons. But the companies building them – and envisioning the warfare of the future – have sharply conflicting views on what their decision-making might look like in future wars.

"Morality is the province of human beings, which is why AI-assisted weapons systems need to be built in a way that extends the judgment and decision-making of the operator rather than wholesale replacing it," says Olaf Hichwa, the co-founder of Neros, a US drone startup.

For Hichwa, drones will not replace human decision-makers, but enhance the abilities of their human pilots.

"A lot of people are looking at autonomy somewhat incorrectly. They're not looking at it through the eyes of the user. They're saying, let's make fully autonomous weapon systems," he says.

"But the reality of warfare is that it's still very much a human-on-human interaction."

One use of AI in warfare, he says, might be to reduce the cognitive burden of the people who have to operate craft such as FPV (first-person view) drones – small craft heavily used in the Ukraine conflict which transmit a view of the battlefield to their human pilots via a video link. In one scenario, a drone could guide itself for the "last mile" of a mission.

"One example of autonomy is … right now, pilots require a lot of very precise training to get the drone to travel along the right angle. Some autonomy could be the user or the human sets a general path and the drone follows that path."

Jon Gruen, the chief executive of Fortem Technologies, which builds systems to defend against drones, has a different view. He points out that the US already uses autonomous defence systems to intercept incoming drones and missiles, and that AI is used "all the way through" in many of these instances, in identifying targets and, at times, launching a weapon at them.

"When you detect something, you then need to identify it. That identification is done autonomously now for the most part."

The same technology can now be used to deploy a weapon, he says, including when to fire a weapon and then guiding it to its target.

Alex Fink, the US chief executive of Swarmer, a US-Ukraine startup that develops software for autonomous drones, says there could be a future scenario where humans might not select individual targets at all but instead designate a "kill box", in which anything in a given area at a given time is fair game for an autonomous system to target.

"A human certifies this area, there are no friendlies, and I guarantee there will be no friendlies in the next 15 minutes. Any vehicle in this area is pre-approved for this period of time," says Fink.

"If a human is able to take that responsibility upon themselves, that they know there will be no friendlies, no bystanders in this area, then that type of mission is possible."

But if the technology is there, the moral and legal consensus is not.
