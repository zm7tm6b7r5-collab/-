- 大意：数百名英国青少年将在政府试点中试验"完全禁用、夜间宵禁、应用限时"三种方案，为"是否禁止 16 岁以下使用社媒"提供证据。
- 段间衔接：开篇直接给出事件主体与目的（"under a government pilot, which will run alongside a consultation"）。
- 论证作用：全文的信息核心——试验先行、立法未定。

**P2｜背景交代（试验设计 1）**
- 大意：全英四个地区共 300 名青少年参与，其中一部分人的社交应用被禁用，以"在家模拟执行社媒禁令"。
- 段间衔接："During the test" 承接上一段，展开具体做法。
- 论证作用：说明试验如何模拟真实禁令。

**P3｜背景交代（试验设计 2）**
- 大意：另一组人夜间被阻断访问或每天限额 1 小时（含 Instagram、TikTok、Snapchat），其余人不受限制作对照；试点为期六周，测量对日常生活的影响。
- 段间衔接："Another group" 并列列举分组，对照逻辑清晰。
- 论证作用：突出"对照试验"的科学性，是全文"先验证再立法"立场的关键支撑。

**P4｜数据支撑**
- 大意：技术大臣 Kendall 称这是"在真实世界检验不同选项"，将依据家庭经验决定下一步；近 3 万名家长与儿童已回应数字福祉咨询。
- 段间衔接：直接引语承接设计，落到政策意图。
- 论证作用：给出官方动机与咨询规模，证明试点不是走过场。

**P5｜递进（独立研究）**
- 大意：与试点并行，一项独立研究将成为全球首个"减少青少年社媒使用影响"的大型科学试验。
- 段间衔接："Alongside the pilots and consultation" 递进引入第二层证据。
- 论证作用：把政策试点升级到科学试验层面，提升可信度。

**P6｜数据支撑（研究细节）**

- 段间衔接：无转折词，承接上段补充细节。

- 段间衔接："It comes as" 引出同时发生的第二件事。

## 四、长难句逐句拆解（6 句）

**S1**
- 原句：Hundreds of UK teenagers will trial social media bans, digital curfews and time limits on apps under a government pilot, which will run alongside a consultation to decide whether the UK should ban access to social media for the under-16s.
- 结构拆解：主句（Hundreds of UK teenagers will trial...）→ under a government pilot 状语 → which 非限制性定语从句（run alongside a consultation）→ to decide whether（宾语从句）。
- 翻译：数百名英国青少年将在一项政府试点中试验"社媒禁令、数字宵禁与应用限时"，试点将与一场咨询并行，以决定英国是否应禁止 16 岁以下人群使用社交媒体。
- 语法点：which 非限制性定语从句；不定式 to decide + whether 宾语从句。
- 仿写：Thousands of commuters will test congestion pricing and restricted lanes under a city pilot, which will run alongside a public survey to decide whether the policy should become permanent.

**S2**
- 原句：Another group will have access blocked overnight, or capped to one hour's use on the most popular social media apps for teenagers, including Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat.
- 结构拆解：will have access blocked / capped（have + 宾语 + 过去分词，使役结构）；including 现在分词短语举例。
- 翻译：另一组人将被夜间阻断访问，或把青少年最常用的社媒应用（包括 Instagram、TikTok 和 Snapchat）限制为每天一小时。
- 语法点：have sth. done 使役结构；过去分词 blocked/capped 并列。
- 仿写：Under the new rules, students will have their phones locked during exams, or restricted to emergency calls only.

**S3**
- 原句：A trial funded by the Wellcome Trust and co-led by the Bradford Institute for Health Research and the University of Cambridge psychologist Prof Amy Orben will examine changes in anxiety and sleep quality, time spent with friends and family, wellbeing, body image, social comparison, school absences and bullying.
- 结构拆解：A trial（过去分词短语 funded by... and co-led by... 作后置定语）→ will examine 后接长宾语列举（changes in... time spent... wellbeing...）。
- 翻译：这项由 Wellcome 基金会资助、Bradford 卫生研究所与剑桥大学心理学家 Amy Orben 教授共同牵头的试验，将考察焦虑与睡眠质量、与亲友相处时间、福祉、身体意象、社交比较、缺勤和霸凌等方面的变化。
- 语法点：双过去分词后置定语；并列宾语的长列举结构。
- 仿写：A study funded by the education ministry and co-led by two Beijing universities will examine changes in reading habits, screen time and students' willingness to seek help.

**S4**
- 原句：The Commons committee on science, innovation and technology is to question whether the available neuroscientific research on the impact of technology on growing brains is sufficient to underpin policy decisions.
- 结构拆解：主语 + is to question（be to 结构表计划安排）→ whether 宾语从句（research... is sufficient to underpin...）。
- 翻译：下议院科学、创新与技术委员会将质询：现有关于技术对成长中大脑影响的神经科学研究，是否足以支撑政策决策。
- 语法点：be to do 表计划；whether 宾语从句；on 介词短语层层限定。
- 仿写：The review panel is to assess whether the existing evidence on remote learning is sufficient to justify a nationwide policy change.

**S5**
- 原句：Although not legally binding, it raises pressure for European legislation amid growing alarm about the mental health risks to children of unfettered internet access.
- 结构拆解：Although not legally binding（省略句，= Although it is not legally binding）→ 主句 it raises pressure for... amid...。
- 翻译：尽管该决议没有法律约束力，但在人们对儿童无限制上网之心理健康风险的担忧日益加剧之际，它推高了欧盟立法的压力。
- 语法点：省略式让步状语从句；amid 介词短语表背景；of 结构（risks to children of unfettered internet access）。
- 仿写：Although not legally binding, the declaration raises pressure for national action amid growing concern about data privacy.

**S6**
- 原句：They are watching their children scroll, message and engage on platforms they know are not safe.
- 结构拆解：watch sb. do sth.（省略 to 的宾补）→ platforms 后接省略 that 的定语从句（they know are not safe，内含 know 的宾语从句）。
- 翻译：他们正看着自己的孩子在明知不安全的平台上滑动屏幕、发消息、参与互动。
- 语法点：感官动词 + 宾语 + 动词原形；嵌套定语从句。
- 仿写：Parents are watching their children spend hours on apps they know are designed to keep them hooked.

## 附录：原文全文（文字版）

**Hundreds of UK teenagers to trial six-week social media curbs for major study**

By Alexandra Topping and Robert Booth, The Guardian, March 25, 2026

Government's pilot ban for under-16s accompanies consultation as peers vote on Australia-style restrictions

Hundreds of UK teenagers will trial social media bans, digital curfews and time limits on apps under a government pilot, which will run alongside a consultation to decide whether the UK should ban access to social media for the under-16s.

During the test, led by the UK government, a proportion of 300 teens across all four nations of the UK will have their social apps disabled, "mimicking the enforcement of a social media ban at home".

Another group will have access blocked overnight, or capped to one hour's use on the most popular social media apps for teenagers, including Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat. Others will experience no restrictions, so the test group's experiences can be compared. The pilots will last six weeks and test how different restrictions affect young people's day-to-day lives.

The technology secretary, Liz Kendall, said it was about "testing different options in the real world". "These pilots will give us the evidence we need to take the next steps, informed by the experiences of families themselves," she said.

Nearly 30,000 parents and children have responded to the government's digital wellbeing consultation, which closes on 26 May.

Alongside the pilots and consultation, an independent study will become the world's first major scientific trial of the impact of reducing social media use among adolescents.

A trial funded by the Wellcome Trust and co-led by the Bradford Institute for Health Research and the University of Cambridge psychologist Prof Amy Orben will examine changes in anxiety and sleep quality, time spent with friends and family, wellbeing, body image, social comparison, school absences and bullying. This study will involve about 4,000 students between the ages of 12 and 15 recruited from 10 Bradford secondary schools.

It comes as MPs launched a separate investigation into the impact of digital devices and social media on children's brain development. The Commons committee on science, innovation and technology is to question whether the available neuroscientific research on the impact of technology on growing brains is sufficient to underpin policy decisions.

"We still know far too little about how these habits affect children's health, wellbeing and cognitive abilities," said Chi Onwurah, the committee chair. The MPs will investigate how dopamine and other hormones released during device use affects brain development and whether the effects of gaming differ from smartphone use.

Ministers have come under increasing pressure to follow Australia's move to ban access to social media sites for under-16s. France, Spain and Indonesia are also considering such a ban. In November, the European parliament passed a resolution on age restrictions. Although not legally binding, it raises pressure for European legislation amid growing alarm about the mental health risks to children of unfettered internet access.

Earlier this month, MPs rejected a proposed ban. On Wednesday, peers will vote on the ban as it is put forward again as an amendment to the children's wellbeing and schools bill tabled by the Conservative peer and former minister John Nash.

Reacting to the pilot, Lord Nash said: "These pilots are simply half measures that once again put the pressure on parents rather than holding big tech accountable and halting the horrific harms that social media is inflicting on a generation."

Bereaved parents have written to parliamentarians urging them to support a ban and stating that they were "deeply concerned" about the consultation. They wrote: "It was announced at the 11th hour, in the days before your previous vote. It proposes an 'expert panel' of academics but leaves little or no space for those on the frontline, those who see the consequences every single day, such as GPs, police officers and others who are dealing with the reality of harm as it unfolds."

They called on parliamentarians to act now, adding: "Right now, across the UK, parents are not waiting for consultations or future reviews. They are watching their children scroll, message and engage on platforms they know are not safe. They are trying to set boundaries without the support of the law. They are fearful, overwhelmed and doing their best to protect their children in a system that is not designed to help them."

The NSPCC chief executive, Chris Sherwood, has said that if the government did not take action to combat "failing" tech companies, a social media ban would be better than the status quo. The charity has called for ministers to force tech companies to keep under-13s off social media, prevent firms from making their platforms highly addictive to teenagers, and use the law to ensure that tech companies keep children safe from taking and sharing illegal images.
